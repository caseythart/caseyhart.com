LifeGraph Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 29, 2026

Summary

LifeGraph is a personal fitness application that runs locally on the user’s own computer. Your heart rate and step data is stored on your computer. It is not sent to me, to any server I operate, or to any third party.

Who is responsible

LifeGraph is developed by Casey Hart (“I” or “me”), an individual. I do not operate a hosted LifeGraph service, and I have no accounts, servers, or databases that hold LifeGraph users’ data.

What LifeGraph accesses

If you choose to connect your Google account, LifeGraph uses the Google Health API to request access to:

  • Heart rate data
  • Step count data

Access is granted through Google’s standard sign-in and consent screen. LifeGraph only receives data you approve there. You can revoke access at any time in your Google Account permissions.

How the data is used

The data is used only to store, query, and display your own fitness information within LifeGraph on your computer. It is not used for advertising, marketing, profiling, or selling, and it is not used to train any AI model.

Where the data is stored

Data is stored locally, in files in RDF Turtle (.ttl) format on the computer where LifeGraph runs. LifeGraph does not upload these files anywhere. It has no analytics, advertising, or tracking, and it does not send usage or crash reports.

Because the data lives on your own computer, its security depends on that computer. I recommend using your operating system’s account protection and disk encryption, and being careful with any backups you make yourself.

Sharing

I do not sell, rent, or share your data. The only outside connection is between LifeGraph on your computer and Google’s APIs, to retrieve the data you authorized. I may disclose information only if required by law, but since I do not hold your data, there is generally nothing for me to disclose.

Google API Services User Data Policy

LifeGraph’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Retention and deletion

Your data stays on your computer until you delete it. To delete it, remove the LifeGraph .ttl data files from your computer. To stop LifeGraph from receiving new data, revoke its access in your Google Account permissions (linked above). Because I do not hold copies, there is nothing to delete on my side.

Changes to this policy

If LifeGraph later becomes available to other people, or is offered as a hosted service that stores data on a server, I will update this policy before any such data is collected. The date at the top shows when it was last changed.
